A reliable content model distinguishes what is missing, what is invalid, what is private, and what
is intentionally illustrative. This demonstration article outlines those distinctions in a reusable
way.

## Separate validity from visibility

Validation answers whether a record has the required shape and values. Visibility answers whether a
valid record belongs in a published list or route.

A draft may be perfectly valid and still remain absent from production. A demonstration may be
public while retaining a conspicuous provenance label.

## Preserve provenance

Illustrative records should not rely on wording alone to signal their status. A dedicated field
allows cards, details, and metadata-adjacent presentation to apply one consistent disclosure.

When genuine information becomes available, the record can be replaced or its provenance changed
without rewriting the layout.

## Give empty states specific meaning

No public documents is not the same as a failed document request. No reading records is not evidence
of absent interests. A filter with zero matches is not the same as an empty collection.

Specific variants produce specific copy and prevent dummy controls from appearing.

## Fail with useful context

Authoring failures should identify the source and field, collect independent issues where possible,
and remain stable across runs. That makes a repository workflow maintainable even when several
records change together.
